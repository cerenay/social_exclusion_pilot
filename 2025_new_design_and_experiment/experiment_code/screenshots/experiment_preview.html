Welcome!

Thank you for participating in this study on decision-making.

Over the course of this study you will earn money. The amount you earn depends on your own decisions, the decisions of other participants, and an element of chance.

Earnings throughout the study are denominated in Experimental Monetary Units (EMU). Your final payoff will be calculated in EMU and converted to pounds at the end of the study, at a rate of 100 EMU = £X.

The study is divided into three parts. Your total earnings will be the sum of your payoffs across the three parts. Instructions for each part will be displayed when you reach it. At the end of the study you will be asked to complete a short survey.

Screen 6 · Part 2 Instructions (all treatments)

Part 2

T1 — no identity, random exclusion

Part 2 of the study now begins.

You will be asked to complete a task that involves moving a slider to a position within a specified numerical range.

You will see a screen displaying five sliders. Each slider is initially positioned at 0 and can be moved up to 100. Moving a slider reveals its current value. Your task is to position each slider within the indicated range.

The task has two versions: an easy version and a hard version. The two versions differ only in the size of the numerical range within which the slider must be positioned.

Another participant will also complete the task. One of you will be assigned the easy task and the other the hard task. The assignment is made randomly by the computer.

You will receive additional EMU for completing the task. Both participants must complete their tasks in order to proceed to Part 3.

Screen 10 · Part 3 Introduction

Part 3

Part 3 of the study now begins. You will be randomly and anonymously paired with another participant.

The independent participant who assigned the tasks in Part 2 does not take part in this part of the study.

You will be asked to make decisions across three rounds. In each round you will be given a fixed amount of EMU and asked to allocate it between yourself and the other participant.

Each round presents a different decision situation, referred to throughout as a scenario. Both participants will record their decisions for every scenario; one participant will be randomly designated as the allocator at the end of Part 3, and that participant's decision in one randomly selected round (and, where applicable, scenario) will determine the payoffs for both.
